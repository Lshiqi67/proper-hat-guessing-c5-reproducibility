\begin{abstract}
We present a computer-assisted argument that the proper hat-guessing
number of the five-cycle is four. The main task is to exclude five arbitrary
local guessing functions that win on every proper five-coloring.
Counting strategy-table entries selects a color guessed between 17 and
25 times: the upper bound follows by averaging, and the lower bound follows
from a classification of two-vertex covers of a missed-pair graph.
Vertex and color relabeling then reduce the problem to 1413 count
representatives, with selected representatives refined into 51 diagonal
answer patterns. A complete local audit with a newly implemented checker
replays all 8613 terminal RUP refutations, including 66,403,934 additions
and every final empty clause. A new input auditor checks all 1413 parent
formulas, all 7344 refined inputs, and all 65,399 valid counter inputs. We explain both
the coverage of all candidate strategies and the extension of each
candidate to its actual Boolean input; these are separate requirements
from checking a refutation. An explicit local witness explains why the
two-occurrence argument alone cannot give the full result. A concordance
links the proof to the organized research and reproducibility records.
This reimplementation audit remains part of the same AI-assisted workflow;
external mathematical review and formal verification of the checking
software remain outstanding.
\end{abstract}
